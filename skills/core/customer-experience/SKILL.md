# Customer Experience — reduce effort · design the journey · build loyalty

Three complementary lenses across the CX lifecycle. Answer from the moves below.

## The three lenses
| Need | Move | Book |
|---|---|---|
| **Win loyalty in service** | Reduce *effort*, don't chase delight | The Effortless Experience |
| **Practical CX plays** | 68 concrete tactics across the journey | How to Wow |
| **Design the service** | Research → ideate → prototype → implement; journey maps, blueprints | This Is Service Design Doing |

## Fast-reference frameworks (the spine)
- **Effort > delight** (Effortless Experience): service mostly *mitigates disloyalty*; delight is overrated/expensive. Reduce the **disloyalty drivers**: repeat contacts, channel switching, transfers, repeating info, robotic service, policy hassles. **CES (Customer Effort Score)** predicts loyalty better than CSAT/NPS in service. Four pillars: **next-issue avoidance** (solve the whole problem, forward-resolution), **easy channels** (don't force channel switching; sticky self-service), **experience engineering** (manage the *feel* via advocacy/positive language — "here's what I *can* do"), **the effort-reducing rep** (give reps control & judgment, not rigid scripts).
- **Service design** (TISDD): the **double-diamond-ish** flow — Research → Ideation → Prototyping → Implementation, all **co-created** and **evidenced**. Core tools: **personas**, **journey maps**, **service blueprints** (frontstage/backstage/support + touchpoints), **system/stakeholder maps**. Design the whole stage, not just the screen.
- **How to Wow**: 68 effortless plays grouped — attract & trust · relationships & engagement · service design & effort · retention/loyalty/recovery · referrals & advocacy · feedback & measurement · culture & employee experience · leadership & change.
